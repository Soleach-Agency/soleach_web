# Cosmetics ad claims in Türkiye: the health-claim boundary

Use this skill when writing, reviewing, or translating **marketing copy for a
cosmetic product sold in Türkiye** — product pages, ad creative, influencer
briefs, marketplace listings, packaging text.

This is the single most common way automated copy generation creates legal
exposure for a beauty brand, because the phrasing that reads as "persuasive" in
English marketing is often the exact phrasing that is prohibited in Turkish
cosmetics regulation.

## The rule

Under the Turkish regulation on health claims — *Sağlık Beyanı ile Satışa
Sunulan Ürünlerin Sağlık Beyanları Hakkında Yönetmelik*, administered by TİTCK
(Türkiye İlaç ve Tıbbi Cihaz Kurumu) — a **cosmetic product may not be
presented as**:

- treating a disease,
- preventing a disease,
- helping to treat a disease,
- correcting physiological functions, or
- having a **pharmacological**, **immunological**, or **metabolic** effect.

Source: https://titck.gov.tr/faaliyetalanlari/denetim/saglik-beyani

A claim that crosses this line reclassifies the product: it is no longer being
marketed as a cosmetic, but as a drug — which requires a different licence
entirely.

## Why this matters more in the AI era

A brand can be careful on its own site and still be misrepresented. Generative
engines do not answer from your site alone — they draw on forums, marketplace
descriptions, reseller pages, and old campaign copy. If a treatment claim is
attributed to your brand from any of those scattered sources, the resulting
sentence is one you never approved, but the user reads it as your brand's
promise.

So the question in cosmetics is not *"am I being mentioned?"* but **"what am I
being mentioned as?"**

The most direct defence is to state clearly, on your own site, in machine-readable
form, both what the product does **and what it does not do**. Every definition
you leave blank gets filled in from somewhere else.

## Rewriting across the line

These are illustrative reformulations, not legal advice, and not an exhaustive
list. The pattern to learn: **describe the cosmetic effect on the appearance or
surface of skin/hair, not a physiological or therapeutic mechanism.**

| Crosses the line | Stays inside it |
| --- | --- |
| "Treats acne" | "Helps reduce the look of blemishes" |
| "Cures eczema / dermatitis" | *(no cosmetic reformulation — this is a drug claim; remove it)* |
| "Prevents hair loss" | "Supports the appearance of fuller-looking hair" |
| "Repairs the skin barrier" | "Helps maintain skin's moisture level" |
| "Anti-inflammatory" | "Soothing / helps calm the look of redness" |
| "Kills bacteria that cause acne" | *(antimicrobial mechanism = drug claim; remove it)* |
| "Regulates sebum production" | "Helps reduce the appearance of shine" |
| "Stimulates collagen synthesis" | "Helps improve the look of skin firmness" |
| "Medical-grade / dermatological treatment" | "Formulated with dermatologist input" *(only if true)* |

Two further traps:

- **Before/after imagery** can carry a therapeutic claim even when the text is
  clean. A visual sequence showing a skin condition clearing reads as a
  treatment claim regardless of the caption.
- **Influencer and UGC copy** is the brand's responsibility. A creator saying
  "this cleared up my eczema" in a paid post is the brand's claim. Brief
  creators on this boundary explicitly; do not assume they know it.

## Practical checklist

When producing or reviewing Turkish-market cosmetics copy:

1. Scan for disease names (akne, egzama, sedef, dermatit, mantar, saç dökülmesi
   as a condition). Any of these near a product benefit is a red flag.
2. Scan for mechanism verbs: *tedavi eder, iyileştirir, önler, onarır,
   düzenler, engeller*. Reformulate to appearance language (*görünümünü azaltır,
   görünümünü destekler*).
3. Scan for pharmacological framing: *anti-inflamatuar, antibakteriyel,
   medikal, klinik olarak tedavi eder*.
4. Check the imagery separately from the text.
5. Publish the honest negative: state what the product is not for. This closes
   the gap that engines would otherwise fill by speculation.
6. Publish per-product factual detail an engine can quote correctly — INCI
   ingredient list, skin-type suitability, usage frequency, known interactions.

## Scope limits

- This covers **Türkiye**. The EU (Regulation 1223/2009 and the Common Criteria)
  and the US (FDA cosmetic vs. drug distinction) draw comparable but not
  identical lines. Do not port Turkish phrasing to other markets unchecked.
- This is a marketing-copy skill, not legal advice. For a specific product,
  claim, or enforcement question, the brand should consult its regulatory
  adviser or TİTCK directly.
